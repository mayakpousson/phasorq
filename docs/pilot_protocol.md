# Five-student usability pilot

This is a recruitment and observation protocol, not a claim that testing has already happened.

## Recruit and observe

Ask five students who did not build PhasorQ and have at least heard of qubits. Invite each to a 15-minute session. Give them the public app link and this prompt: **"Choose one experiment, compare the two configurations, tell me what happened, and save a report you could use later."** Do not teach the interface during the task. Record whether they finish, elapsed time, where they hesitate, and what they say. Ask permission before recording a session.

After the task, ask: **"If we increase the number of measurements, will that remove hardware noise? Why?"** Then ask what they would use the result for, what they distrust, and what was missing. Avoid leading questions about whether they liked the app.

## Success criteria

- At least four of five finish the core task without assistance.
- At least four explain that more measurements can reduce sampling variation but cannot remove systematic device errors.
- At least three return independently to try another experiment within two weeks.

Track the last criterion through voluntary follow-up, not invisible usage tracking. Do not collect names in public experiment records. Report all five outcomes, including failures, and fix the most common obstacle before sharing a product claim.

## What to publish

Publish a short, anonymized case study with the protocol, completion times, common mistakes, changes made from feedback, and limits of a five-person sample. Keep simulation benchmark results and user outcomes separate.
